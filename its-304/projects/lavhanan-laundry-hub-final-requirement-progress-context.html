Decisions

  1. Follow the template's chapter structure exactly: Chapters I–IV, no invented Chapter V. If the instructor requires a conclusion/recommendations, add it only after confirming.
  2. V-model goes in Chapter III as the methodology figure; it is Figure 1 in the template's list.
  3. ERD vs Data Entity Diagram: ERD (Chen) = conceptual entities/relationships; Data Entity Diagram = physical table/column schema. Explain both, do not merge them.
  4. The 10 template figures are the minimum. Add context, class, activity, deployment, and component diagrams to the List of Figures if they are used; the template's Chapter IV asks for them even though its figure list omits some.
  5. Be honest about the prototype:
    • It is a responsive Symfony web app, not a native Android app. Android users open it in a browser. A native Android client is future work / part of the design scope, not a delivered artifact.
    • SMS is demonstrated with a mock outbox transport (sms://outbox). Production needs a real gateway (Twilio/Vonage) and internet.
    • Thermal printing uses the browser's 72 mm print layout, not a direct ESC/POS driver.
    • No online payment, barcode/RFID, digital scale, or multi-branch support.
  6. Use real data where possible: actual services/prices, shop address/hours, demo flow, test counts. Use placeholders only for facts we do not have (group full names, real owner name, exact submission month).
  7. Diagrams are editable artifacts: draw in draw.io/diagrams.net or PlantUML, export PNG/PDF, and keep the source files next to the paper.
  8. Screenshots come from the live app, seeded with demo data; hide or blur anything that looks like a real customer's personal data.
  9. Screenshot presentation: the Symfony debug badge/toolbar is deliberately left visible in captures as evidence the screens were tested live; only real customer personal data is blurred (see Decision 8).

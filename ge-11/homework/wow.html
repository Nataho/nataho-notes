Feature / CategoryMale Reproductive SystemFemale Reproductive SystemSimilarities and Differences
Primary Sex Characteristics & Gonads- Gonads are the testes.
- Produce sperm (germ cells) and testosterone (androgen).		- Gonads are the ovaries.
- House ova/eggs (germ cells) and produce estrogen and progesterone.		- Similarity: Both use primary sex organs (gonads) present at birth to make germ cells and sex hormones.

- Goal: Both work together to produce offspring and create new life.
External Genitalia (Outer Hardware)- External organs are the penis (head, shaft, root), scrotum, prepuce (foreskin), corona, frenulum, urethral opening, and perineum.ng the glans penis.- External organs form the vulva (mons veneris, labia majora and minora, prepuce, vestibule, clitoris, openings, and perineum).- Similarity: Both have external sex organs (genitals) present at birth. Both have a prepuce (clitoral hood or penis foreskin).

- Difference: The perineum space between the genitals and anus is longer in males than in females.
Internal Parts & Pathways (Inner Hardware)- Includes testes, vas deferens, seminal vesicles, ejaculatory ducts, prostate gland, and urethra.

- Seminal vesicles (70%) and prostate (30%) produce alkaline fluid to feed sperm and protect them from acid.		- Includes ovaries, fallopian tubes, uterus (womb), cervix, and vagina.

- Fallopian tubes carry eggs to the uterus; the vagina is a collapsible canal that engorges during arousal.
- Similarity: Both have inner organs and tubes to carry germ cells.

- Difference: The male urethra carries both urine and semen out of the body. In females, the urethral opening (urine) and vaginal opening are separate.
Germ Cell Output- Produces sperm in virtually unlimited amounts over a lifespan.- Born with ~1 million immature ova, but only 400–450 mature in a lifetime.- Difference: Males make a continuous, unlimited supply of sperm, while females are born with a set number of eggs that stop maturing around age 45–50 (menopause).
Hormones & Cycles- Driven mainly by testosterone from the testes.

- Has no monthly cycle.		- Driven by estrogen and progesterone from the ovaries.

- Governed by the hypothalamus in a monthly cycle lasting 24 to 42 days.		- Difference: Females experience a monthly cycle with menstrual bleeding when an egg is unfertilized. Males do not have a monthly cycle.
Secondary Sex Characteristics (Puberty Changes)- Broader shoulders and deeper chest.

- Voice breaks/deepens.

- Taller with more body/facial hair and higher body water.		- Breast enlargement (glandular and fatty tissue).

- Widening of hips and pelvis.

- Generally shorter with a higher proportion of body fat.		- Similarity: Both develop secondary sex characteristics during puberty when gonads ramp up hormone production.

- Difference: Males build broader shoulders and deeper voices, while females develop breasts and wider hips.

Challenge 2

Stage 1: Birth (Primary Characteristics)

  • Female Specs
    • External genitals (vulva, labia, clitoris) present
    • Shorter Distance between genitals and anus
    • Ovaries house around 1 million immature ova
  • Male Specs
    • External genitals (penis, scrotum, foreskin) present
    • Greater distance between genitals and anus

Stage 2: Childhood

  • Both Sexes
    • Physical and emotional changes toward adulthood
  • Female
    • Immature ova remain dominant in ovaries

Stage 3: Puberty (system update)

  • Female Changes
    • Breasts form
    • Hips and pelvis widen
    • First period (menarche) at ages 11 to 15
    • Monthly cycle begins via hypothalamus
  • Male Changes
    • Genitals develop
    • Pubic and body hair grows
    • Voice breaks and deepens
    • Growth spurt with broader shoulders
    • Testosterone drives sperm creation

Stage 4: Adulthood and aging

  • Female path
    • Only 400 to 450 ova mature across the lifespan
    • Monthly Cycle lasts 24 to 42 days
    • Menopause occurs around ages 45 to 50
    • Two X chromosomes lead to longer lifespan
  • Male Path
    • Unlimited sperm production over lifespan
    • No monthly cycle
    • Facial hair and increased baldness risk
    • Single X Chromosome increases colorblindness risk

Brief History of BPO in the Philippines

One of the most robust sectors in the Philippines right now is the information technology and business process outsourcing (IT-BPO) sector. More than just a lucrative enterprise, the IT-BPO sector has also proven to be the largest and fastest-growing industry in the country. There is no denying its major role in providing quality jobs and encouraging economic activity and investment even in areas outside Metro Manila.

Of course, the IT-BPO industry was not an overnight affair. Credit is due to the handful of individuals who saw the potential in the business in its early stages. Among them are the likes of Rainier "Bong" Borja, who is often regarded as the most articulate voice of the Philippine BPO industry;

Karen Batungbacal for her early involvement and series of contact center firms; and

Secretary Mar Roxas, who, during his tenure as secretary of the Department of Trade and Industry, saw the potential of the industry, reached out to key leaders to promote foreign investment, and helped shape the landscape of BPO into what it is today.

Brief History of BPO Sector in the Philippines

Timeline Highlights

  • 1992 — First Contact Center: Frank Holz, under the Accenture group, created the first contact center in the Philippines.
  • 1995 — Special Economic Zone Act: The Philippine Congress passed this act, lowering area requirements for development and offering tax incentives to attract foreign investors.
  • 1997 — First BPO Multinational: Sykes Asia established operations, becoming the first multinational BPO company in the Philippines.
  • 1999 — First Call Center: Jim Franke and Derek Holley founded eTelecare, which became widely recognized as the country's first call center.
  • 2000 — GDP Contribution: The BPO industry accounted for 0.075% of the Philippines' total GDP.
  • 2001 — Expansion & Job Creation: PeopleSupport, a US-based outsourcing center, restructured its business operations to move to the Philippines, generating approximately 8,400 jobs.
  • 2003 — Convergys Expansion: Convergys Corp. opened two call centers in the country. Chosen alongside India by company president Jack Freker, this formed a key part of their global growth plan.
  • 2005 — Global Market Share: The Philippines captured 3% of the global BPO market, which represented 2.4% of the country's GDP.
  • 2006 — PLDT Ventus & Growth: PLDT Ventus emerged at the forefront of the local BPO landscape, helping boost the Philippine domestic economy by 5.4%. Around 11,000 individuals were employed across North America, Europe, and Asia.
  • 2010 — BPO Capital Title: The Philippines earned the recognition of being the "world's BPO capital", employing over 525,000 people in call centers and generating $8.9 billion in revenue.
  • 2011 — Top Job Provider: The sector became one of the fastest-growing job providers in the private sector, generating $11 billion in revenue, employing 638,000 Filipinos, and comprising 4.9% of total GDP.
  • 2012 — Continued Expansion: Industry revenues reached 5.4% of total GDP, maintaining a growth rate of around 46% annually since 2006.
  • 2013 — Milestone Numbers: Revenue climbed to $15.5 billion, with full-time employment reaching 900,000 Filipinos.
  • 2016 — Long-Term Projection: Projections estimated the industry would generate 1.3 million new jobs with a 17% annual growth rate.

A Glimpse Into the Past, Present, and Future of the BPO Industry in the Philippines

Timeline

  • 1992: Accenture establishes the first contact center in the Philippines.

  • 1995: The Philippine Economic Zone Authority (PEZA) is established.

  • 1999: eTelecare, the first call center company in the country, is founded.

  • 2000: The BPO sector contributes 0.075% to the Philippine GDP.

  • 2001: Establishment of the Contact Center Association of the Philippines (CCAP).

  • 2002: Proposal to revise Republic Act 7916 to extend tax perks to PEZA-registered BPOs.

  • 2005: The BPO industry captures 3% of the global market.

  • 2006: The BPO sector experiences a 46% annual growth rate.

  • 2008: The Philippines surpasses India in BPO growth.

  • 2010: The Philippines officially earns the title of the BPO capital of the world.

  • 2019: The BPO industry thrives despite pandemic-related shutdowns.

  • 2020: According to PEZA, IT-BPO investment pledges increased by 37% from January to July 2020.

  • 2021: The outsourcing industry continues to grow despite the challenges of the COVID-19 pandemic.

  • 2022: IBPAP actively lobbies for the permanent adoption of hybrid work.

  • 2023: The country boasts more than 700 BPO companies.

  • 2024: More than 850 BPO companies.

  • 2028: 59 billion target revenue.

  • 2030: AI roadmap estimated a $92 billion GDP contribution by 2030.

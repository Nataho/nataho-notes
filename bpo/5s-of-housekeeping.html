5S of Housekeeping

Last updated: Nov 6, 2026

5S of Housekeeping

5S is a workplace organization / housekeeping method that uses five Japanese words, all starting with the letter S: seiri, seiton, seiso, seiketsu, shitsuke. It was developed in Japan as part of the Toyota Production System (anglicized as Lean) and is usually the first lean method organizations implement. The goal: "a place for everything and everything in its place."

5S reduces waste and optimizes productivity by maintaining an orderly workplace and using visual cues (signs, labels, floor markings, color codes) for more consistent operational results.


The 5S methodology (Nikita Klyuchko / Respublika Narodnaya, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA)


The 5S "onion" diagram (Rafabitu, Wikimedia Commons, public domain)

The Five S's

JapaneseEnglishWhat it means
SeiriSortSeparate needed from unneeded items (tools, parts, materials, paperwork) and discard the unneeded
SeitonSet in OrderNeatly arrange what is left — a place for everything and everything in its place
SeisoShineClean the workplace and equipment thoroughly
SeiketsuStandardizeStandardize the way the first three S's are done so cleanliness is maintained
ShitsukeSustainDiscipline — keep following the first four S's without being told

1. Seiri (Sort)

  • Sorting through all items in a work area and removing everything not needed for current operations.
  • Make work easier by eliminating obstacles.
  • Best-known tool: red tagging — a red tag is placed on every item that isn't needed (or isn't in the right location/quantity) so it can be evaluated and removed.
  • Remove items that are broken, unusable, or only occasionally used.

2. Seiton (Set in Order)

  • Put all necessary items in the optimal place for fulfilling their function — organize for efficient use.
  • Arrange tools/parts so they can be easily picked for use; put frequently used items near the operator.
  • Prevents loss and wasted time searching; makes it easy to spot missing or misplaced items.
  • Uses labels, shadow boards, floor marking, and color coding so items are easy to find AND easy to return.
  • Principle: "Can See, Can Take Out, Can Return."

3. Seiso (Shine)

  • Clean the workplace completely — sweep, dust, polish.
  • Use cleaning as inspection: while cleaning, you spot problems, leaks, and equipment deterioration early.
  • Identify and eliminate the sources of dirt and grime (remove the need to clean).
  • Divide areas into cleaning zones and assign ownership/responsibilities.

4. Seiketsu (Standardize)

  • Standardize the processes by which Seiri, Seiton, and Seiso are done every day to keep the workplace in perfect condition.
  • Tools: job cycle charts, visual cues (signs, placards, scoreboards), scheduled "five-minute" 5S periods, checklists, and 5S audit sheets.
  • Assign 5S job responsibilities and integrate them into regular work duties.

5. Shitsuke (Sustain)

  • Form the habit of always following the first four S's without being told — discipline.
  • The hardest S: human nature resists change and drifts back to "the old way."
  • Sustained through regular training, routine 5S audits, management setting the example, and making 5S part of daily routine (a way of life).

Why 5S matters

  • Reduces non-value-added activities and waste (time searching, motion, inventory).
  • Improves safety, morale, and quality; makes school/factory/office conditions problems visible at a glance.
  • Creates the foundation for other lean methods (TPM, just-in-time, Six Sigma, Kaizen).
  • Improves customers' first impressions of a workplace.

Variations

  • Some practitioners add a sixth S: Safety (establish and practice safety procedures).
  • Toyota traditionally uses just 4S (Seiton and Seiso combined; audits replace Shitsuke) — but now uses 5S as standard.
  • Two main frameworks: Osada and Hirano (Hirano structured 5S as sequential improvement steps).
  • Although it originated in manufacturing, 5S also applies to offices, warehouses, workstations, and even knowledge work (information/software in place of physical product).

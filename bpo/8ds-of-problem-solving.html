8Ds of Problem Solving

Last updated: Nov 14, 2026

8Ds — The Eight Disciplines of Problem Solving

What are the 8Ds?

8D (Eight Disciplines) is a team-oriented problem-solving method developed at Ford Motor Company. It is used by quality engineers to identify, correct, and eliminate recurring problems: instead of patching a symptom, the team finds the root cause, verifies it, and puts a permanent corrective action in place. It follows the logic of the PDCA cycle (Plan–Do–Check–Act) and is a standard in the automotive, assembly, and other industries that need thorough, structured, team-based problem solving.

Where the 8Ds came from

  • In 1986, executives of Ford's Powertrain Organization (transmissions, chassis, engines) wanted a methodology where teams — design engineering, manufacturing engineering, and production — could work on recurring chronic problems together.
  • The manual was documented as Team Oriented Problem Solving (TOPS), first published in 1987 and piloted at Ford World Headquarters in Dearborn, Michigan.
  • The ideas of corrective actions and containing nonconforming items trace back to a U.S. Department of Defense standard, MIL-STD-1520 (Corrective Action and Disposition System for Nonconforming Material, 1974, cancelled in 1995).
  • In the late 1990s, Ford revised the process into Global 8D (G8D) — the current global standard for Ford and many companies in the automotive supply chain. G8D added the D0 gateway step and the concept of escape points.
  • Today the 8Ds are used far beyond the car industry — in food manufacturing, health care, high-tech manufacturing, and by quality teams in service industries such as BPO when investigating escalations and client complaints.

The 8 Disciplines (D0–D8)

D0 — Prepare and Plan (Emergency Response Actions)

Plan for solving the problem and determine the prerequisites; provide emergency response actions. In Global 8D, D0 is the gateway: the team documents the symptoms that started the effort, records any Emergency Response Actions (ERAs) already taken, and answers assessing questions to decide whether a full 8D is even needed.

D1 — Use a Team

Establish a team of people with product and process knowledge. Teammates provide new perspectives and different ideas when it comes to problem solving — a small cross-functional team, not one person.

D2 — Describe the Problem

Specify the problem in quantifiable terms using 5W2H: who, what, where, when, why, how, and how many / how much. A common tool at this step is the Is / Is Not worksheet, which narrows down what the problem is and is not.

D3 — Develop Interim Containment Plan

Define and implement containment actions to isolate the problem from any customer while the real fix is being developed. Containment actions may not be needed depending on where the problem occurred in the life cycle of the product.

D4 — Determine and Verify Root Causes and Escape Points

Identify all applicable causes that could explain why the problem occurred, and verify or prove each one. Common tools: Five Whys and Ishikawa (fishbone) diagrams. The team also finds the escape point — the earliest control point in the control system that should have detected the problem but failed to do so (why did nobody catch it earlier?).

D5 — Verify Permanent Corrections (PCs)

Using pre-production programs, quantitatively confirm that the selected correction will resolve the problem for the customer — prove the fix actually works before rolling it out.

D6 — Define and Implement Corrective Actions

Implement the best corrective actions and validate them with empirical evidence of improvement, including actions that address the escape point.

D7 — Prevent Recurrence

Modify the management systems, operation systems, practices, and procedures to prevent this and similar problems from happening again (for example, updating the FMEA).

D8 — Congratulate the Main Contributors to Your Team

Recognize the collective efforts of the team — the team needs to be formally thanked by the organization.

Tools used inside the 8Ds

  • Ishikawa (fishbone) diagrams — cause-and-effect mapping (D4)
  • Pareto charts — separate the vital few causes from the trivial many
  • 5 Whys — keep asking why until the root cause appears (D4)
  • 5W2H — who, what, where, when, why, how, how many (D2)
  • Check sheets, histograms, scatter plots — data collection and analysis
  • Statistical process control (SPC) and design of experiments
  • FMEA (Failure Mode and Effects Analysis) — an 8D can jump-start its fishbone from an FMEA, and 8D findings get fed back into the FMEA for future planning
  • Flowcharts / process maps — understanding the process where the problem lives

Why teams use the 8Ds (benefits)

  • An effective, structured approach to finding the root cause instead of treating symptoms.
  • Proper actions to eliminate root causes and a permanent corrective action that actually holds.
  • Studying the escape point improves the control system's ability to detect the failure if it ever occurs again.
  • The prevention loop (D7) fixes the systems that allowed the problem to exist in the first place.

8Ds vs. 5S

5S of HousekeepingThe 8Ds
TypeProactive, daily workplace organizationReactive, structured problem solving
PurposeKeep the workplace sorted, clean, and standard so problems are less likelyInvestigate and permanently fix one recurring problem that already happened
CadenceContinuous habitRun once per problem, as a project

They complement each other: good 5S prevents many problems, and the 8Ds fix the ones that still slip through.
